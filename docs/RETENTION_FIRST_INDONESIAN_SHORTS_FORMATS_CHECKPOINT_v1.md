# Video_AI — Retention-First Indonesian Shorts Formats Checkpoint v1.0

- **Date:** 2026-11-09
- **Status:** PLANNED
- **Scope:** Creative strategy checkpoint; no format has been validated by published pilot data.
- **Purpose:** Explore distinctive short-form entertainment formats rooted in recognizable Indonesian everyday situations, with explicit reasons for viewers to stay until the payoff.

## 1. Strategic correction

Earlier broad themes such as everyday social rules, informal systems, and cultural observations were judged too ambiguous as standalone video concepts. Familiarity with a topic does not itself create retention.

The new design principle is:

**Start with a concrete viewer question or challenge, build visible progression or escalating evidence, and reserve a meaningful payoff for the ending.**

Every format should aim to provide:
1. An immediately understandable visual hook (target: first 1–2 seconds).
2. A concrete question, conflict, challenge, or decision.
3. Progressive escalation, new evidence, or meaningful changes in stakes.
4. A payoff that resolves or reframes the opening question.
5. A repeatable episode engine that can generate many cases without becoming formulaically predictable.

These are creative design criteria, not guaranteed platform-performance rules.

## 2. Candidate formats

### A. SIDANG WARGA — Who Is in the Wrong?

**Premise:** A short fictional or dramatized neighborhood dispute is presented as a case. Multiple parties give their version; evidence or a late reveal changes the audience's initial judgment; viewers deliver the verdict.

**Example case:** Who is responsible when the household water gallon is empty and nobody replaces it?

**Suggested beat structure (illustrative 20–30 seconds):**
- 0–2s: Show the dispute and pose the verdict question.
- 2–8s: Introduce the parties and their competing claims.
- 8–17s: Reveal testimony, context, or evidence.
- 17–25s: Reveal the decisive detail and resolve the case.
- End: Ask viewers to judge, with a specific comment prompt.

**Other case seeds:** A motorcycle blocks a narrow alley; the last snack at a gathering disappears; borrowed sandals are mixed up at a celebration; neighborhood cash is short; someone leaves shared chores unfinished.

**Retention engine:** Viewers form an early opinion, then wait to see whether new evidence overturns it.

### B. MISI MUSTAHIL ORANG INDONESIA — Impossible Everyday Missions

**Premise:** A character attempts a clear, visual task under increasingly difficult constraints.

**Example:** Can all the groceries be carried on one motorcycle without anything falling?

**Beat structure:** State the target → show the initial attempt → introduce escalating constraints → show near-failure or adaptation → reveal success/failure.

**Other mission seeds:** Organize a large number of guests' sandals in a tiny space; fit a set of event supplies into a limited area; complete a household task with restricted tools or budget.

**Retention engine:** Visible progress, rising difficulty, and an unknown outcome.

### C. TEBAK SEBELUM TERUNGKAP — Guess Before the Reveal

**Premise:** Viewers solve a small visual mystery using clues introduced throughout the video.

**Example:** Which of three characters secretly took the last fried snack?

**Beat structure:** Present suspects and mystery → reveal observable clues → introduce a misleading or easily overlooked detail → invite a guess → connect the clues in the reveal.

**Retention engine:** Active participation and the desire to check whether one's guess was correct.

### D. 1 BENDA, 5 BENCANA — One Object, Five Trials

**Premise:** An everyday object faces a sequence of increasingly difficult fictional tests or comedic situations.

**Example:** Can a plastic chair survive five increasingly chaotic situations at a neighborhood gathering?

**Beat structure:** Establish the object and question → trials 1–3 → escalate the challenge → final trial → reveal the outcome.

**Other objects:** Flip-flops, buckets, umbrellas, thermoses, fans, stools, reusable shopping bags.

**Retention engine:** A visible countdown of trials and curiosity about the final limit.

**Integrity note:** If AI-generated or fictional, present it as comedy or animation—not as a real product test or evidence of actual durability.

### E. PILIHAN YANG SALAH — Every Choice Has a Consequence

**Premise:** Viewers choose between options in a familiar everyday dilemma; the story then reveals the consequences of each decision.

**Example:** You have Rp20,000 to get through the evening. Which food option do you choose?

**Beat structure:** State the constraint → present clear options → let viewers choose → dramatize consequences → reveal the most workable outcome under the stated assumptions.

**Other situations:** Choosing where to park, what to carry in heavy rain, how to divide food at a gathering, or how to handle a shared bill.

**Retention engine:** Personal investment in a decision and comparison between the viewer's choice and the ending.

### Recommended first candidate: SIDANG WARGA

Reasons:
- The conflict can be understood quickly without extensive exposition.
- Everyday neighborhood situations provide a broad, repeatable case pool.
- Competing perspectives and late evidence naturally create a reason to wait for the verdict.
- A recurring courtroom/meeting identity and cast can make the series recognizable.
- It can be produced as stylized AI animation, provided the fictional nature is clear where needed.

**Working series identity:** WARGA vs WARGA — Setiap Masalah Punya Tersangka.

This is a recommendation for the first pilot, not an approved claim of market uniqueness or proven superiority.

## 4. Pilot validation plan

Do not immediately produce a large batch. Start with three pilot episodes using different dispute types but the same core format.

Suggested pilots:
1. The empty water gallon: who should replace it?
2. The blocked alley: who must move the motorcycle?
3. The missing last snack: who took it, and was it actually wrong?

Keep the episode structure and approximate duration consistent. Avoid changing multiple major creative variables at once.

### Metrics to record
- Viewed vs. swiped away (where platform provides it).
- Audience retention by second and average percentage viewed.
- Completion rate, where available.
- Replays, where available.
- Comments per view and the substance of verdict-related discussion.
- Shares and saves.
- Production time and cost per episode.

Compare each pilot against the channel's relevant baseline and against the other pilots. Do not interpret one successful or unsuccessful upload as conclusive evidence.

## 5. Editorial and production guardrails

- Treat stories as fictional/dramatized unless based on verified real events and appropriately sourced.
- Do not portray a whole region, ethnic group, religion, class, or generation as having one universal behavior.
- Avoid humiliating identifiable private individuals or inviting viewers to target real people.
- Build fair cases: give each side understandable motives and avoid arbitrary endings.
- Make the decisive clue visible or inferable; do not resolve the story through information withheld unfairly from the audience.
- Avoid fake claims that AI-generated challenges or object tests happened in real life.
- Keep the opening question and ending payoff aligned.
- Do not confuse comments or controversy with healthy retention or audience value.

## 6. Competitive differentiation status

**VERIFICATION_REQUIRED.** No claim is made that these formats have never been attempted by any creator. The current checkpoint captures creative directions and a pilot hypothesis, not a completed competitor audit.

Before committing to a long-term series identity, conduct a targeted review of Indonesian and international short-form creators for:
- The exact recurring premise and episode structure.
- Hook, escalation, evidence/reveal, and payoff mechanics.
- Visual identity, character system, and episode packaging.
- Whether the format is common, emerging, or underdeveloped in the target audience/language.

The differentiation opportunity should be defined as a specific combination of premise, storytelling mechanics, recurring characters, and production identity—not an unverifiable claim of being the first.

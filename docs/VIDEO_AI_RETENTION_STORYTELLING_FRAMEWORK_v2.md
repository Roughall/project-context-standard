# Video_AI — Retention & Storytelling Framework v2.0

- **Status:** APPROVED AS WORKING GUIDANCE
- **Date:** 2026-11-06
- **Scope:** Short-form narrative video production and post-publication evaluation for Video_AI.
- **Basis:** Comparative analysis of four user-provided social-video examples and captions. This document records the resulting project decisions; it is not a claim that the creators' diagnostic labels are empirically proven.

## 1. Core principle

**Fast hook, clear progression, satisfying payoff, evidence-led evaluation.**

A short video should make its premise understandable immediately, keep curiosity developing through meaningful story changes, and resolve the promise it made. Retention analytics are diagnostic clues, not definitive explanations of audience behavior.

## 2. Story design rules

### 2.1 Hook quickly
- Make the opening visual legible without requiring prior context.
- Start with the unusual event, visible problem, character reaction, or immediate stakes.
- Avoid non-essential logo, greeting, exposition, or slow setup.
- Do not reveal the entire payoff at the start merely to satisfy a generic “show the payoff in 1–2 seconds” rule. Show the premise or a compelling glimpse; preserve the complete resolution when the story benefits from it.

### 2.2 Develop curiosity; do not pad
- Each beat should change the situation, increase stakes, reveal information, deepen emotion, or set up a payoff.
- Delaying an answer is useful only while the story continues to offer meaningful development.
- Do not withhold an answer artificially, repeat the same beat, or stretch a simple idea just to increase duration.

### 2.3 Deliver the payoff
- Resolve the central promise clearly and satisfyingly.
- A final gag or tag is optional; keep it only when it adds humor, emotion, meaning, or natural replay value.
- Do not let a CTA interrupt the emotional or comedic resolution.

### 2.4 Cut by function, not by a fixed percentage
Remove material that does not contribute to:
- comprehension,
- curiosity,
- conflict or escalation,
- emotion,
- payoff, or
- intentional rhythm.

“Cut 30%” is a heuristic from the source videos, not a mandatory formula.

## 3. Flexible short-form beat map

For a 10–20 second story, use this as a starting point—not a rigid timing template:

| Beat | Approximate timing | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Visual hook | 0–2 s | Make the situation immediately understandable and interesting |
| Conflict | 2–6 s | Show what the character wants or what goes wrong |
| Escalation | 6–12 s | Change or complicate the situation |
| Payoff | 12–17 s | Resolve the central promise |
| Optional tag | 17–20 s | Add a meaningful final gag, emotional note, or loop |

Shorter videos may combine beats. Timing must follow the actual story, not force the story into arbitrary slots.

## 4. Retention graph interpretation

Read the graph as **where viewers leave or remain**, then inspect the corresponding footage to form hypotheses. Never assign a cause from graph shape alone.

| Observed pattern | Possible interpretation | What to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp early decline | Opening may be unclear, slow, mismatched to the promise, or visually weak | First frame, opening action, title/thumbnail promise, audience-source context |
| Drop after the hook | The story may not develop after attracting attention | Does the next beat introduce a new action, question, consequence, or reveal? |
| Gradual decline | Some viewers lose interest as the video progresses | Repetition, pacing, clarity, visual change, unnecessary beats |
| Late decline | Payoff may be delayed, the ending may be weak, or viewers may consider the story complete | Payoff timing, ending, CTA placement |
| High or above-100% average viewed | Replays/loops or very short duration may contribute | Video duration, loop structure, average view duration, retention curve |
| High views but low follows/subscribers | The video may work as a standalone view without communicating a reason to follow | Recurring character/series identity, consistency of promise, profile/channel conversion |

These are hypotheses to test, not categorical labels such as “bad hook,” “weak topic,” or “trash content.” A flat graph at a low level is not automatically good. A late drop is not automatically a weak CTA. A high retention percentage is not by itself proof of story quality.

## 5. Evaluation framework

Evaluate each release using multiple signals:
1. **Distribution:** views and traffic sources.
2. **Opening:** early retention and the first seconds of the video.
3. **Viewing quality:** average view duration, average percentage viewed, and the shape of the retention curve.
4. **Response:** meaningful engagement, shares, saves, comments, and sentiment where available.
5. **Audience development:** follows/subscribers relative to views.
6. **Series value:** whether the content reinforces a recognizable character, format, or recurring promise.

Interpret platform metrics in their own context. Do not directly equate metrics with different definitions across platforms. Do not infer the actual viewer identity from demographic reports alone. One viral post is not sufficient evidence to select a niche or declare a repeatable format.

## 6. Experiment and iteration protocol

For each meaningful test:
1. State one hypothesis (for example: “a clearer first-frame action will reduce the initial drop”).
2. Change one major variable at a time where practical.
3. Keep the story, audience context, platform, and measurement window documented.
4. Compare the new result with a relevant prior baseline; avoid treating unrelated videos as controlled experiments.
5. Record both the metric change and the actual creative difference.
6. Keep, revise, or reject the hypothesis based on evidence.

Do not optimize solely for upload count. A consistent publishing cadence can create learning opportunities, but repeated publishing without review is not a learning system.

## 7. Application to Video_AI / Bobo

For Bobo and other mostly dialogue-free character stories:
- Make the conflict visually understandable without relying on spoken explanation.
- Use clear character goals, readable actions, expressive reactions, and a visible consequence.
- Preserve character and environment continuity across generated clips.
- When production limits require multiple generations (for example, two clips of up to 10 seconds), design the seam deliberately: screen direction, character position, prop position, motion, lighting, and action at the end/start must match.
- Do not add shots or camera changes merely for variety; every angle should improve clarity, emphasis, comedy, or emotion.

### Example: “The Pizza Escape”
- **Hook:** the pizza begins rolling away just as Bobo is about to take it.
- **Conflict:** Bobo chases it.
- **Escalation:** the pizza stops in front of a hungry kitten.
- **Payoff:** Bobo shares the pizza.
- **Optional final gag:** the last slice rolls away and Bobo reacts/chases again.

This example demonstrates the principles; it is not a mandatory template for every Bobo episode.

## 8. Production acceptance checklist

Before publishing, check:
- [ ] The first visual is understandable and creates a reason to watch.
- [ ] The video starts with meaningful action; no unnecessary intro.
- [ ] Every beat changes the situation or adds story value.
- [ ] Curiosity develops naturally; no artificial delay or padding.
- [ ] The central promise receives a clear payoff.
- [ ] CTA, if used, does not interrupt the payoff.
- [ ] Character, prop, environment, and motion continuity are preserved.
- [ ] Any clip transition has been checked visually.
- [ ] The video is evaluated using multiple platform-appropriate signals after publication.

## 9. Evidence and limitations

- This framework is a synthesis of four user-provided short-form videos/captions and the Video_AI production context discussed in the project conversation.
- Claims about exact retention thresholds, mandatory “1–2 second” timing, fixed 30% trimming, or guaranteed algorithmic distribution are **not** established by those examples.
- Platform-specific metric definitions and current recommendation-system behavior should be checked against current official documentation before drawing technical conclusions.
- Treat the framework as working guidance that should evolve with Video_AI's own measured results.
